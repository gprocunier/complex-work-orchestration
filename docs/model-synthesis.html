Model Synthesis And Adjudication

CWO can ask several models for independent reviews, but it does not average their answers into truth. Synthesis reports the evidence; the architect decides what to accept.

Why It Exists

Independent reviews are useful because different model families notice different risks. One model may catch a docs-flow problem, another may challenge a security boundary, and a third may find a missing validation gate. The value comes from preserving those independent findings before combining them.

The danger is treating agreement as permission. CWO keeps model returns as advisory evidence until they are evaluated, dispositioned, synthesized, and adjudicated by the architect.

Input Quality

Synthesis is only as useful as the returns it receives. CWO tracks input dispositions before synthesis: accepted, accepted-with-modification, salvage-only, open-risk, missing, timed-out, process-held, rejected, quarantined, or boundary-tainted.

Salvage-only means useful but limited. A return may provide wording ideas, alternate framing, or risk prompts without counting toward the minimum usable primary inputs. Gemini architecture critique is treated this way by default unless the architect explicitly upgrades a specific evaluated finding.

Boundary-tainted returns are not silently blended into the result. If a reviewer used unapproved context, claimed shell work it was not allowed to perform, or crossed the sharing boundary, that return must be rejected or quarantined before synthesis can be trusted.

Provider-policy limitations are also carried as provenance, not erased. When opaque intervention risk or provider conflict is present, the Guardrails provider-policy workflow keeps peer review and architect adjudication in the path. Pending peer review uses implementation_blocked and open-risk synthesis handling instead of masquerading as substantive quarantine.

For security-sensitive synthesis, CWO can also require zero-trust consensus. Accepted primary inputs provide structured claims, CWO compares those claims across independent trust domains, and unresolved divergence blocks implementation conversion until the architect adjudicates it.

Contradiction Example

A Codex architect proposes a public-docs refactor. One reviewer says three deep-dive pages are the clearest structure. Another says to merge guardrails into the synthesis page to reduce navigation. Synthesis does not vote. It records the disagreement, checks which recommendation matches the operator requirement, and asks the architect to decide.

Accepted signal Use problem-first intros, examples, accessible diagrams, and top-nav restraint.
Rejected signal Collapse the guardrails page when the operator explicitly requested a dedicated page.
Architect decision Keep three pages, but make the guardrails page concrete and evidence-focused.

Adjudication

The synthesis artifact should say what the reviewers agreed on, where they disagreed, which claims lacked evidence, what risks changed, which inputs were salvage-only or missing, and what plan revisions are recommended. It should preserve provider provenance and should not make the release decision.

  • Synthesis reports; it does not decide.
  • Outside models provide evidence; they do not gain authority.
  • Research claims need source locators, citation spans or excerpts, contradiction handling, and reflection before they become usable synthesis input.
  • Zero-trust consensus reports cross-domain claim divergence; it does not turn agreement into validation.
  • Accepted findings become plan changes only after architect adjudication.
  • Process-held findings stay visible as open-risk evidence until peer review or architect adjudication resolves the hold.
  • Rejected or quarantined findings stay in the record as boundary evidence.

When To Use It

Use synthesis for high-risk architecture, security-sensitive planning, publication work, provider-conflict review, airgapped model substitution choices, or any task where independent critiques are expected to materially improve the plan. Do not use it for narrow edits where one Beads task and focused validation are enough.

Continue with the multi-expert review flow, use Zero-Trust Consensus when structured cross-domain claim comparison is needed, or use Reference for exact executor IDs and helper commands.
